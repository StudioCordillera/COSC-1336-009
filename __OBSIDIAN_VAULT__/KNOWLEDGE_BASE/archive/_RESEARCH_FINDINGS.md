## RESEARCH TASK 1: RELATIONAL COGNITIVE FRAMEWORKS

**Goal:** Find 3-5 frameworks for organizing knowledge relationally

**Primary Search Areas:**
- Concept mapping methodologies (Novak, Cañas, Safayeni)
- Knowledge graph construction for education
- Zettelkasten method (Luhmann, Ahrens)
- Semantic network theory (Collins & Quillian, Anderson)
- Personal knowledge management systems
- Network theory applied to learning

**Key Questions to Answer:**
1. How do these frameworks define "atomic" knowledge units?
2. What types of relationships do they recognize? (hierarchical, associative, causal, etc.)
3. How do they handle prerequisite knowledge chains?
4. What empirical evidence supports their effectiveness?
5. How have they been implemented in digital tools?
6. What are the cognitive principles underlying each approach?

**Specific Papers/Books to Find:**
- Novak & Cañas: "The Theory Underlying Concept Maps and How to Construct Them"

- Anderson: "A Spreading Activation Theory of Memory"
- Cañas et al.: "Concept Maps: Integrating Knowledge and Information Visualization"
- Look for recent implementations in Obsidian/Roam Research communities

## RESEARCH TASK 2: PROGRAMMING EDUCATION FRAMEWORKS

**Goal:** Find frameworks specifically for teaching programming concepts

**Primary Search Areas:**
- Bloom's Taxonomy applied to CS education (ACM papers)
- SOLO Taxonomy for programming (Biggs & Collis)
- Computational thinking frameworks (Wing 2006, Brennan & Resnick 2012)
- PCK (Pedagogical Content Knowledge) for CS
- Threshold concepts in CS education
- Worked example effect (Sweller, Atkinson, van Merriënboer)
- Schema acquisition in programming (Soloway, Adelson)

**Key Questions to Answer:**
1. What are the "threshold concepts" in programming education?
2. How should programming concepts be sequenced cognitively?
3. What's the evidence for worked examples vs problem-solving?
4. How do experts vs novices organize programming knowledge differently?
5. What role do schemas play in programming expertise?
6. How does transfer work between programming contexts?

**Specific Papers to Find:**
- Wing (2006): "Computational Thinking"
- Brennan & Resnick (2012): "New frameworks for studying and assessing computational thinking"
- Thompson et al.: "Bloom's Taxonomy for CS Learning Outcomes"
- Sweller: "Cognitive Load During Problem Solving"
- Soloway & Ehrlich (1984): "Empirical Studies of Programming Knowledge"
- du Boulay (1986): "Some Difficulties of Learning to Program"
